run on ubuntu-server, CPU reference backend throughout. The two phases

## What the numbers mean

On this backend a block-forward row costs about 250 s (verify totals
divided by rows), while a single-token decode step costs about
45 s: the multi-token path is roughly 5.5x the per-row price of the
single-token path, before speculation's arithmetic even starts. A
7-row verify therefore costs about 1650-1740 s to buy back at most
7 tokens, so the speculative loop cannot win on the CPU reference
backend, and this run does not pretend otherwise (phase totals:
2250.5 s plain vs 10231.7 s speculative for the same 16 tokens).
The mechanism is the deliverable: token identity at temperature 0
against the real files, a real 31 percent acceptance rate from the
trained draft at the sweep's depth 6, and the checkpoint/rewind
round running end to end on the real geometry (52 layers, window
2048 rings included). The speed case belongs to the GPU resident
path, where one submission evaluates the whole block (the remaining
half of TODO T-9.6, and T-9.7's sweep on this stack).

## Incident record (kept because it cost real time)

- The first attempt ran both phases in one process and was
  OOM-killed (kernel log 2026-10-30 13:30 UTC, 59.5 GB anon RSS):
  a duplicated launch put two instances into the same staging
  directory and log file, and their interleaved writes made the
  log unreadable as evidence. The runner was split into per-phase
  processes (commit 499ecce).
- The second attempt's phase B was OOM-killed at 56.9 GB RSS eight
  minutes in (borrowed embedding/head tables are 10.8 GB of f32 and
  the draft's dequantized weights about 10 GB on top of the
  target's footprint). Relaunched with MALLOC_ARENA_MAX=2 and
  MALLOC_TRIM_THRESHOLD_=0, phase B peaked at 44.5 GB early and
  completed; allocator arena retention was roughly 12 GB of the
  earlier peak.
- Two process launches were also corrupted by shell quoting on the
  prompt (word-split through the ssh layer turned mode and stream
  arguments into prompt words). The fix that ended the class:
  launch from a script file and verify /proc/<pid>/cmdline before
  believing a run.
- Intermediate monitoring readouts in this environment proved
  unreliable more than once (numbers that later direct reads of the
  same files contradicted). Every figure in this note was taken
  from a direct read of the final log files.

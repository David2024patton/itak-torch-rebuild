# GPU Kernel Performance: Shared-Weight Small-Batch Quantized Matmul on RADV / RDNA3 (RX 7900 XTX)

Mission: Rust + wgpu (Vulkan) inference, GGUF Q4_K/Q5_K/Q6_K LLM matmul. Per-row GEMV streams weights at up to 676 GB/s. Two shared-weight small-batch kernels (wide-tile double-buffered; purpose-built, each weight tile once per dispatch through workgroup memory) reached only 70–130 GB/s effective and were slower overall, despite correctness. llama.cpp Vulkan does far better on the same card/model class.

Research date: **2026-11-04**. Flags: **[verified live]** = page opened and read in the research browser this run; **[index]** = search-index result/snippet only, not opened live. Treat index-only claims as leads to re-check on the target machine.

## Summary

**The headline arithmetic (analysis, from the mission numbers + the card spec).** The RX 7900 XTX is a 960 GB/s card [index: https://siliconcat.net/gpu/amd-radeon-rx-7900-xtx — 384-bit, 960.0 GB/s]. So:

- GEMV at 676 GB/s = **~70% of peak** — a credible cold-streaming result for a dequantising GEMV.
- Small-batch at 70–130 GB/s = **~7–13% of peak, 5.2–9.7× below the engine's own GEMV.** That is not a “shared-memory tax” of tens of percent; it is a kernel that is *not keeping the memory system busy* — too few resident waves, too few outstanding loads, stalls serialising on LDS/barriers, spills, or redundant loads. Every published case below with this signature was diagnosed as one of those, never as “workgroup memory is inherently slow.”
- Small batch bounds the prize anyway: reusing a weight tile for `B` columns divides weight traffic per token by `B`, but if the shared kernel runs at 1/6 the streaming rate of `B` separate GEMV dispatches, it loses until `B > ~6`. Splitting awkward batches into power-of-two chunks that *do* scale is exactly what a production llama.cpp fork ended up doing (see §4).

**Most likely causes, in the order to test them (each is cheap to falsify, see §1 and §4):**

1. **Occupancy collapse from the wide tile** — accumulators + double-buffered LDS. Published: BM≥128 tiles 2.4× slower from occupancy collapse; a 32 KiB accumulator spill capped occupancy at 2 waves, fixing it (scratch 32768→0) raised occupancy to 6 [verified live: https://papers.hanzo.ai/pdfs/refutation-driven-perf.pdf].
2. **Too few workgroups for 96 CUs.** The 7900 XTX has 96 CUs [index: https://videocardz.net/amd-radeon-rx-7900-xtx]. A wide-tile dispatch over e.g. a 4096-row matrix with 128-row tiles is only 32 workgroups — under one wave per CU — whereas per-row GEMV launches thousands. ZINC's RDNA4 profiling: large matmuls 93.2% of bandwidth, a small (m=32) matmul **2.7%** — “small matmuls can't saturate the memory subsystem” [index: https://github.com/zolotukhin/zinc/blob/HEAD/docs/RDNA4_TUNING.md].
3. **LDS latency/bank conflicts, not DRAM, as the stall source.** On a measured Vulkan matvec, `s_waitcnt` LDS waits (`lgkmcnt`) outnumbered VMEM waits ~5:1 (~1167 vs ~230); the author re-diagnosed it as “LDS-latency + occupancy bound, NOT HBM-latency bound” [index: https://github.com/dhavli/llamacpp-vega-gfx900/commit/6576600e548ad7ffbc006b10f03cf03593fa922e].
4. **Compiler-generated redundant weight loads.** Mesa issue #15132: the *same* Q4_K GEMV SPIR-V compiled by RADV/ACO vs AMDVLK/LLPC — ACO emitted **25% more `buffer_load`s and 184% more scalar loads**, 507 vs 583 GB/s effective (15% decode regression), blamed on SGPR allocation clobbering loop-invariant buffer descriptors [index: https://gitlab.freedesktop.org/mesa/mesa/-/issues/15132]. Inspect the ISA before redesigning the kernel.
5. **Batch-size-specific pathology.** On RADV/RDNA3.5, batched mat-vec at **3, 5 and 6 columns is several times slower than at 1, 2 and 4** [verified live: https://github.com/halo-box/strix-llama.cpp]. If the failing batches are non-power-of-two, this alone may explain it.
6. **Dequant-through-LDS expansion.** Q4_K is 144 bytes per 256 weights = 0.5625 B/weight [index: https://www.aicassindra.com/blogs/transformer_math/tm_gguf_deep.html]. Dequantising to f16 in workgroup memory writes+reads 2 B/weight — **3.6× the DRAM bytes, through LDS, plus barriers** — before any multiply. llama.cpp's fast paths avoid exactly this: scalar MMQ keeps weights packed and uses integer dot product on Q8_1-quantised activations; the 2026 int8 coopmat path “probes and directly accesses coopmat values instead of going through shmem” [verified live, commit list in https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/27952].

**No source was found publishing the exact 70–130 GB/s WGSL/wgpu number on a 7900 XTX** — see Could Not Verify. The failure *mode* is well documented; that specific data point appears to be the requester's own.

## Findings

### 1. Profiling and debugging tooling for Vulkan compute on RADV/RDNA3

Work in this order — cheapest, most decisive first. This ordering is itself published practice: in the Hanzo campaign, “free static rejection → bit-exact oracle → sustained per-op timing → in-engine wall” was the gate ladder, and static shader statistics “disqualified a large fraction of hand-tried variants (register spills, LDS over the occupancy budget) before they ever reached the GPU” [verified live: https://papers.hanzo.ai/pdfs/refutation-driven-perf.pdf].

**Occupancy arithmetic to apply to the shaderstats output** (ROCm's published method): occupancy is the minimum of the VGPR limit and the LDS limit, `occ_lds = floor(65536 / LDS_per_workgroup)`, with waves-per-workgroup from the workgroup size [index: https://rocm.docs.amd.com/en/docs-6.1.1/how-to/llm-fine-tuning-optimization/optimizing-triton-kernel.html]. Double-buffering exactly doubles the LDS term; a wide accumulator tile raises the VGPR term. Compute both for the GEMV and for each failed kernel — in the published cases (§4) this arithmetic alone predicted the collapse.

### 2. Driver-level and toolchain-level levers

#### Drivers: there is effectively one, and one diagnostic ghost

- **RADV is now AMD's Linux Vulkan driver, full stop.** AMD discontinued AMDVLK in **September 2025** in favour of RADV, and dropped its proprietary Vulkan driver from Radeon Software for Linux (25.20+) [index: https://github.com/GPUOpen-Tools/radeon_gpu_profiler/blob/master/docs/source/index.rst, quoting the discontinuation; discussion reference https://github.com/GPUOpen-Drivers/AMDVLK/discussions/416 via https://wiki.nixos.org/w/index.php?title=AMD_GPU&diff=26387&oldid=8142]. Final AMDVLK benchmarks (Phoronix, Dec 2025, Mesa 26.0-devel RADV vs AMDVLK 2025.Q2.1) found RADV ahead in raster/compute generally, AMDVLK's last stronghold being ray tracing [index: https://www.phoronix.com/review/radeon-radv-amdvlk-final].
- **But AMDVLK remains a legitimate A/B diagnostic, not a future.** Because AMDVLK compiles SPIR-V with LLPC/LLVM rather than ACO, running the *same* wgpu binary under `AMD_VULKAN_ICD=AMDVLK` vs `=RADV` (both ICDs installed) [index: https://emirror.d0a.io/wiki/wiki.gentoo.org/wiki/AMDVLK.html] isolates “ACO codegen” from “kernel design” in one environment variable. Mesa #15132 (§4) is proof this comparison can be worth 15% on exactly this workload class — and also proof the fix, if found there, belongs upstream in ACO or in shader restructuring, since AMDVLK is unmaintained.
- **Mesa version is a lever.** New RGP performance counters for RDNA3, coopmat fixes, and ACO scheduling work land per-release; the requester's Mesa version was not in the mission, so pin and record it (`vulkaninfo`, `RADV_DEBUG=info`-style startup output) and re-test on current Mesa before concluding the kernel is at fault. **Could not verify** which Mesa the failing runs used.
- **RADV runtime knobs worth A/B-ing** [all verified live: https://docs.mesa3d.org/envvars.html]:
  - `RADV_PERFTEST=cswave32` — force wave32 for compute (GFX10+). Interacts with §3: llama.cpp's RDNA coopmat/MM paths deliberately force subgroup size 32 (below).
  - `RADV_DEBUG=llvm` — force the LLVM backend instead of ACO for *all* shaders. Blunt (compile times, other shaders) but a one-run test of the ACO hypothesis.
  - `RADV_DEBUG=nocache` while iterating, so a stale shader cache cannot silently keep an old binary; `syncshaders`/`fullsync` only for debugging, never for timing.
  - driconf options exist beyond env vars (e.g. a `radv_cooperative_matrix2_nv` toggle reported for Mesa 25.2's partial `VK_NV_cooperative_matrix2`) — **[index, weak source]** http://portallinuxferramentas.blogspot.com/2025/06/radv-driver-adds-support-for.html — flag: not confirmed in official Mesa docs this run; do not rely on coopmat2 on RADV/RDNA3.

#### Shader compiler backends and flags

- RADV's pipeline is SPIR-V → NIR → **ACO (default)** or LLVM. ACO is “developed specifically for RADV… better performance and shader compilation times compared to LLVM” as the default for most stages [index: https://deepwiki.com/erkia/mesa/3.1.2-radv-shader-compilation — secondary summary of Mesa source; the env-var facts above are verified live in Mesa's own docs]. ACO bisection flags: `ACO_DEBUG=noopt`, `nosched`, `nosched-ilp`, `force-waitcnt` [verified live: https://docs.mesa3d.org/envvars.html]. If `nosched` or `force-waitcnt` changes the rate materially, scheduling/latency-hiding — not bandwidth — is the story.
- **Front-end matters as much as back-end:** the same SPIR-V from different front-ends gives ACO different NIR. Aliased buffer views of the same binding are a concrete hazard: Mesa #15132's shader accessed weights “through multiple aliased SSBO views (`data_a`, `data_a_packed16`, `data_a_packed32`, all binding=0)” [index: https://gitlab.freedesktop.org/mesa/mesa/-/issues/15132]. WGSL/Naga commonly generates such multi-view access for `array<u32>` vs `array<f32>` reinterpretation of one buffer — check the SPIR-V (`spirv-dis`, from SPIRV-Tools/Vulkan SDK) and the ACO asm for duplicated descriptors/loads.

#### Shader authoring routes beyond WGSL

WGSL through Naga is the constrained route: no cooperative-matrix types, no integer-dot-product intrinsic as such, no explicit workgroup-memory layout/aliasing, no buffer-device-address, and workgroup defaults that must be explicitly raised (WebGPU defaults: `maxComputeWorkgroupStorageSize` 16384, `maxComputeInvocationsPerWorkgroup` 256 [index: https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/GPUSupportedLimits] — native wgpu exposes higher adapter limits, but only if the code requests them via `Limits`; a kernel designed against the defaults is LDS-starved by construction).

| Route | How it plugs into a Rust engine | What it unlocks over WGSL |
|---|---|---|
| **SPIR-V passthrough in wgpu** | wgpu v25 unified this as `Device::create_shader_module_passthrough` (replacing `create_shader_module_spirv`), taking a `ShaderModuleDescriptorPassthrough` [index: https://github.com/gfx-rs/wgpu/releases/tag/v25.0.0]. Compile GLSL/HLSL offline, ship `.spv`, bypass Naga entirely on Vulkan. | Everything Vulkan/SPIR-V exposes: `GL_KHR_cooperative_matrix`, `GL_KHR_shader_subgroup_*`, integer dot product (`SPV_KHR_integer_dot_product`), `VK_KHR_workgroup_memory_explicit_layout`-style 16-byte vector copies into shared memory, push constants/specialisation as in llama.cpp. This is the lowest-risk escape from WGSL: host code stays wgpu. |
| **GLSL via glslang/shaderc — the llama.cpp route** | llama.cpp compiles GLSL to SPIR-V offline with `glslc` and generates variants with its `vulkan-shaders-gen` tool over specialisation constants (`BLOCK_SIZE`, `BM`, `BN`, `WM`, `WN`, `TM`, `TN`…) [index: https://deepwiki.com/wtwver/llama.cpp/6.4-vulkan-backend]. | Proven on this exact problem (§3). Also note the failure mode: coopmat shader generation depends on the local `glslc` reporting `GL_KHR_cooperative_matrix` support — a build-time probe, not just a runtime one [index: https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/issues/13801]. |

| **rust-gpu (Rust → SPIR-V)** | `rustc_codegen_spirv` compiles Rust kernels to SPIR-V for Vulkan [index: https://rust-gpu.github.io/blog/2025/07/25/rust-on-every-gpu/]. | Kernel logic in Rust, shared with CPU tests. #### Subgroup and cooperative-matrix status on RADV / RDNA3

- **Subgroups in wgpu:** landed as native-only WGSL features in **wgpu 0.20**: `subgroupBallot/All/Any/Add/Mul/Min/Max/And/Or/Xor`, exclusive/inclusive scans, `Broadcast/BroadcastFirst`, `Shuffle/Down/Up/Xor`, gated by `Features::SUBGROUP` (Vulkan/DX12/Metal), `SUBGROUP_BARRIER` (Vulkan/Metal), `SUBGROUP_VERTEX` (Vulkan) [index: https://github.com/gfx-rs/wgpu/releases/tag/v0.20.0]. Tracking issue #5555 records remaining divergences from the WebGPU spec (missing quad ops, `subgroupElect`) [index: https://github.com/gfx-rs/wgpu/issues/5555]. So a WGSL kernel *can* do subgroup reductions/shuffles today — a shared-weight kernel that instead reduces/stages through workgroup memory is leaving this unused.
- **Subgroup size on RDNA:** llama.cpp's device classification treats AMD RDNA3 as **subgroup size 32–64 with integer-dot acceleration**, GCN as 64, RDNA2 as 32–64 without int-dot [index: https://deepwiki.com/wtwver/llama.cpp/6.4-vulkan-backend]. RDNA WMMA is a **wave32** instruction (all 32 lanes cooperate on 16×16×16) [verified live: https://rocm.blogs.amd.com/software-tools-optimization/rdna-fp16-gemm/README.html]. llama.cpp uses `VK_EXT_subgroup_size_control` “to ensure full subgroups for coopmats” and the 2026 int8 path does “only force subgroup size 32 on AMD RDNA” / “use wave32” [index: https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/13626; verified live commit list: https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/27952]. If the wgpu kernel implicitly runs wave64, every WMMA-shaped or shuffle-shaped pattern pays double-issue and halved effective lanes — check the compiled wave size in shaderstats/RGP.
- **Cooperative matrix (coopmat1): supported on RADV since Mesa 23.3, GFX11+ only**, using RDNA3 WMMA [index: https://www.phoronix.com/news/RADV-VK_KHR_cooperative_matrix; Mesa release-note commit https://gitlab.freedesktop.org/sree/mesa/-/commit/519b8ee74e2f558a3c39150c8f01f5eeb64fdb3a lists “VK_KHR_cooperative_matrix on RADV/GFX11+”]. AMD's Windows driver added it for RX 7000 in 23.7.1 (July 2023) [index: https://www.amd.com/en/resources/support-articles/release-notes/RN-RAD-WIN-VULKAN.html]. In llama.cpp the runtime check is visible in its device log as `matrix cores: KHR_coopmat` [index: https://yifei.sg/jekyll/update/2025/08/27/building-llamacpp-strix-halo.html] — replicate that probe (`vkGetPhysicalDeviceCooperativeMatrixPropertiesKHR` via vulkaninfo/passthrough) on the target box; **the exact type/shape list RADV exposes on gfx1100 was not enumerated from a primary source this run** and must be queried on-device, as coopmat path selection in llama.cpp is gated per-type (e.g. on RDNA4, Q4_K/Q5_K dense deliberately stay on the FP16 path while other types use int8 [index: https://github.com/nextscript/auto-tuner/blob/HEAD/docs/llama-b11160-audit.md]).
- **Integer dot product (DP4A path):** `VK_KHR_shader_integer_dot_product` exposes accelerated packed 4×8-bit dot (`integerDotProduct4x8BitPackedSignedAccelerated` etc.) as device properties [index: https://docs.vulkan.org/refpages/latest/refpages/source/VkPhysicalDeviceShaderIntegerDotProductProperties.html]. llama.cpp's MMQ path pre-quantises activations to Q8_1 and uses integer dot in `mul_mmq.comp` [index: https://deepwiki.com/wtwver/llama.cpp/6.4-vulkan-backend]. For *small* batch this matters more than coopmat: it multiplies packed weights directly, with no f16 LDS staging at all.
- **Coopmat2:** NVIDIA-led (`VK_NV_cooperative_matrix2`), motivated explicitly by llama.cpp/ggml's experience that SIMT decomposition is “dominated by shepherding data between lanes or reading from shared memory” [index: https://dev.vulkan.org/user/pages/09.events/vulkanised-2025/T47-Jeff-Bolz-NVIDIA.pdf]. RADV support is at best partial/opt-in (weak source above). **Do not plan on coopmat2 on RDNA3.**

### 3. Kernel-design techniques that achieve high weight-streaming rates on RDNA

#### What llama.cpp actually does — three different kernels, not one “matmul”

This is the central structural lesson. llama.cpp does **not** run small batches through its tiled GEMM:

1. **Decode / very small batch — `mul_mat_vec` (+ `mul_mat_vec_q` integer-dot variant).** One workgroup per output row(s), threads cooperating over K, `K_PER_ITER` = 8 for quantised types, reduction by subgroup arithmetic or subgroup-with-no-shared-memory variants (`_subgroup`, `_subgroup_no_shmem`) chosen per device [index: https://deepwiki.com/wtwver/llama.cpp/6.4-vulkan-backend]. A tuning PR “doubled the number of rows per workgroup… only… for AMD and subgroup size 64” [index: https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/13626] — rows-per-workgroup is a tuned, vendor-gated constant, not a guess. The strix fork's chunking (§4) further shows llama's *batched* mat-vec is dispatched at column counts that scale (1, 2, 4…) rather than one kernel for arbitrary N.
2. **Prompt / larger batch, scalar path — `mul_mmq`.** Activations are quantised to Q8_1 once (`quantize_q8_1.comp`), then integer dot product consumes **packed weights directly** — no dequant-to-f16, no LDS weight tile. This is the path whose absence in ZINC was costed at “a factor of two to four on the largest prefill dispatches” on RDNA4 [index: https://zolotukhin.ai/blog/2026-04-19-why-q8-1-activations-are-the-next-rdna4-prefill-unlock/].
3. **Prompt, matrix-core path — `mul_mm` / `mul_mmq_cm1`.** Three-level tiling: block tile `BM×BN×BK` staged in shared memory (`buf_a`/`buf_b`), warp tile `WM×WN`, thread tile `TM×TN`; padded `SHMEM_STRIDE` to avoid bank conflicts; vectorised `LOAD_VEC_A/B` global→shared copies; coopmat loads/mul-add where available [index: https://deepwiki.com/wtwver/llama.cpp/6.4-vulkan-backend]. The **Aug–Sep 2026 int8 coopmat1 MMQ for RDNA3/RDNA4** (PR #27952, merged; supports Q4_0/Q4_1/Q5_0/Q5_1/Q8_0/Q3_K/**Q4_K/Q5_K/Q6_K**/MXFP4/NVFP4/IQ4_NL) is a catalogue of the techniques in one commit series: *double buffering, larger workgroups, wave32, BK_STEP 2→4, preload scales, coopmat-load-first-then-WMMA, workgroup scheduling for cache proximity, restructure for VGPR use, skip inactive tiles, 4-byte loads where possible, probe/directly access coopmat values instead of going through shmem* [verified live: https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/27952]. Its benchmarks are prompt-processing (MUL_MAT) focussed — e.g. Q4_K MUL_MAT geomean 5535→4749 µs vs the fp16 coopmat path on Strix Halo — i.e. coopmat wins at prefill sizes, which is **not** evidence it wins at batch 4–8.

#### The RDNA GEMM optimisation ladder (AMD's own walkthrough)

AMD's ROCm blog builds an FP16 WMMA GEMM on RDNA3 (PRO W7800, gfx1100 — same gfx11 generation as the 7900 XTX) step by step, measuring registers and LDS at each step [verified live: https://rocm.blogs.amd.com/software-tools-optimization/rdna-fp16-gemm/README.html]:

- **Step 2 (the big jump):** larger tile + **wide 128-bit memory copies** + double buffering + staged commit, applied together.
- **Step 3:** avoid LDS bank conflicts — “consecutive rows of a tile can map to the same LDS bank and serialize; padding or permuting the layout spreads accesses apart.”
- **Step 4:** buffer resource descriptors for global loads (out-of-bounds returns zero, removing edge-case divergence).
- **Step 5:** deepen the K slice — hold several WMMA K-steps in LDS, “doing more arithmetic between global-memory copies and barriers” (i.e. fewer barriers per byte).
- **Step 6:** reorder block launch order for cache reuse across concurrent workgroups.
- **Step 7:** **interleave prefetches with WMMA** — issue global loads between matrix instructions so latency hides behind arithmetic.
- **Step 8:** **revisit double buffering** — “with interleaving in place, test whether two LDS buffers are still worth their occupancy cost, or whether one buffer and more resident workgroups is faster.” Double buffering is a hypothesis to A/B, not a default. The Hanzo ladder independently shipped a **single-buffer** rung as a win, and its refutation log records “BM≥128 tiles (2.4× slower, occupancy collapse)” and f16-accumulator variants as “LDS-limited, not register-limited” [verified live: https://papers.hanzo.ai/pdfs/refutation-driven-perf.pdf].
- Also fixed in that walkthrough: **CU mode vs WGP mode** changes how many waves of a workgroup execute together and how much LDS they get (WGP: 4 waves over one contiguous LDS; CU: 2 waves over half) — workgroup geometry interacts with the mode in ways a WGSL author cannot directly control, another reason to measure occupancy rather than assume it, ibid.

**A concrete A/B sequence for the requester** (each step falsifies one §Summary hypothesis):
1. Run the small-batch kernel as `B` separate GEMV dispatches, then as chunks of 2 and 4 (strix pattern). If chunked GEMV beats the shared kernel, the shared kernel — not “small batch” — is the problem.
2. `RADV_DEBUG=shaderstats,asm` on all three kernels; tabulate VGPR, scratch, LDS, `buffer_load` count/width. Fix any spill or <128-bit weight load first.
3. Same kernel, `RADV_DEBUG=llvm` and (if installed) `AMD_VULKAN_ICD=AMDVLK`. A large delta = compiler, not design.
4. Halve the tile / drop double-buffering / deepen BK, one at a time, with wgpu timestamps, sustained and cold. AMD step 8 and Hanzo both predict single-buffer may *win* at this size.
5. `MESA_VK_TRACE=rgp` + `MESA_VK_TRACE_PER_SUBMIT=1` on the surviving variant; read occupancy, instruction timing, and LDS bank-conflict counters in RGP before any further redesign.
6. Only then: SPIR-V passthrough prototype of the MMQ pattern (Q8_1 activations + packed integer dot, no LDS weight staging) for batch ≤8, and the coopmat1 pattern for batch ≥32 — matching llama's own split, rather than one kernel for all batches.

### 4. Published evidence of this specific failure mode

**Yes — the mode is documented from at least four independent directions, though no source matches the exact WGSL/7900 XTX/70–130 GB/s triple (see Could Not Verify).**

1. **Batched mat-vec non-scaling on RADV (closest structural match).** strix-llama.cpp (community fork for RDNA3.5 `gfx1151`, RADV): “Batched mat-vec at 3, 5 and 6 columns is several times slower than at 1, 2 and 4 on RADV here, which hits speculative decoding hard (it verifies at exactly those batch sizes).” Fix shipped: split such batches into column counts that scale; `GGML_VK_MMV_NO_SPLIT=1` restores the single upstream dispatch for comparison. Diagnosed at the level of “the slow `NUM_COLS` shader variants” — i.e. per-batch-size shader variants behave non-monotonically on RADV [verified live: https://github.com/halo-box/strix-llama.cpp]. *Transfer caveat: gfx1151 APU, unified memory — not a discrete 7900 XTX.*
2. **Compiler-caused bandwidth waste on Q4_K GEMV (Mesa #15132).** “ACO: 15% decode regression vs LLPC on bandwidth-bound GEMV — SGPR allocation causes 25% more buffer loads (RDNA4/gfx1201)”: RADV/ACO 507 GB/s vs AMDVLK/LLPC 583 GB/s effective on llama.cpp Qwen3-30B-A3B Q4_K_M decode; same SPIR-V, ACO emitted 25% more VRAM loads and 184% more scalar loads; occupancy was identical and maximal in both, refuting the occupancy explanation *for that case*; root cause assigned to ACO's SGPR allocator reusing buffer-descriptor registers for exec-mask backup [index: https://gitlab.freedesktop.org/mesa/mesa/-/issues/15132]. Lesson: “streaming far below peak” can be pure codegen, invisible without ISA diffing. Related: llama.cpp issue #20848 — IQ3 dequant shaders using 64 VGPRs on AMD limiting occupancy [index, summarised in https://github.com/cecil-the-coder/llama-cpp-moe-flash/blob/HEAD/docs/research-survey.md].
3. **LDS-bound, not DRAM-bound, matvec (Vega/gfx900 measurements).** ISA evidence from a llama.cpp fork: LDS `s_waitcnt` waits ~1167 vs VMEM ~230; VGPR 63 → 4 waves/SIMD (40% occupancy) on wave64; citing #20848's 64 VGPR / 4 waves / ~208 GB/s vs 40 VGPR / 6 waves / 229 GB/s on the same silicon. Later rounds refuted three “obvious” fixes in turn — VGPR diet/higher occupancy (the *lower*-occupancy variant was faster), pure-ALU expansion instead of LDS (20% slower), wider LDS reads via a byte LUT (11% slower, bank conflicts) [index: https://github.com/dhavli/llamacpp-vega-gfx900/commit/6576600e548ad7ffbc006b10f03cf03593fa922e; https://github.com/dhavli/llamacpp-vega-gfx900/commit/62ff5c09a4ddc824fe492174f6d3e91e1f3e2ff1]. Lesson: at this scale the interactions are non-intuitive and only per-change measurement separates them — and “more occupancy” is not axiomatically better.
4. **The Hanzo refutation campaign (RDNA3.5, Vulkan, Q4_K_M/Q6_K, vs llama.cpp).** N=1 observational paper, explicitly bounded as such. Vulkan prefill started 11.6× behind llama.cpp on the same box/model; the wins were: giving Q6_K a tiled path after a per-op profiler showed it at 76% of prefill time (212→704 tok/s); packed-LDS and coopmat rungs; a single-buffer rung. Refuted: BM≥128 tiles (2.4× slower, occupancy collapse), BK=64 on the coopmat path, the max-occupancy tile family (“best occupancy, worst time”), f16 accumulators (LDS-limited), and warm-microbench conclusions (dp4a matvec 255 GB/s cache-hot vs 97 GB/s in-engine; a coopmat GEMM written off at 0.07–0.20× of dp4a in warm microbench became the shipping prefill default once its *access pattern* was made coalesced) [verified live: https://papers.hanzo.ai/pdfs/refutation-driven-perf.pdf]. This is the strongest published evidence that (a) a from-scratch Vulkan kernel can sit an order of magnitude behind llama.cpp on RDNA for mundane, findable reasons, and (b) the reasons are found by per-op profiling + shader stats, not by redesign.
5. **Small-matmul bandwidth collapse as a general RDNA/Vulkan phenomenon.** ZINC's RDNA4 tuning notes, profiling with `GGML_VK_PERF_LOGGER=1`: vocab-output matmul 93.2% of bandwidth, MoE experts (m=512) 59.6%, **small matmul (m=32, k=2048) 2.7%** — “Large matmuls are near bandwidth-optimal. Small matmuls can't saturate the memory subsystem.” Also: raw Vulkan dispatch overhead measured at 0.016 µs/dispatch GPU time, so dispatch overhead does *not* explain small-kernel slowness there [index: https://github.com/zolotukhin/zinc/blob/HEAD/docs/RDNA4_TUNING.md]. *Caveat: RDNA4, and that document mixes tuning lore with measurements — its claim that `RADV_PERFTEST=coop_matrix` is required for coopmat was **not** corroborated in current Mesa env-var documentation (which lists no such PERFTEST flag) and should be treated as obsolete/unverified.*
6. **Double-buffering and LDS widening regressing (RDNA4 fp8 GEMM study, HIP not Vulkan).** A hand-built oracle-verified GEMM found double-buffering the K-loop “regressed in all 4 variants”, widening LDS reads via a transposed layout dropped 87→50 TF, and the residual gap was occupancy gated by VGPR pressure (256 VGPR → spill → 6–33 TF on big tiles) [index: https://github.com/kmbandy/llama.cpp/blob/HEAD/docs/superpowers/results/2026-06-15-rdna4-fp8-gemm-p0-p1-results.md]. Independent corroboration that the two techniques the requester tried first — wide tile + double buffering — are precisely the ones that can backfire on RDNA.
7. **Why coopmat1 itself was considered awkward (Vulkanised 2025, NVIDIA's Jeff Bolz).** Coopmat1 GEMM requires manual pipelining, shared-memory staging, tiling and bounds-checking, “bogged down in addressing math… type punning (want 16B loads, but elements are only 2B)” — the motivation for coopmat2 [index: https://dev.vulkan.org/user/pages/09.events/vulkanised-2025/T47-Jeff-Bolz-NVIDIA.pdf]. Explains why llama's coopmat shaders are large, generated, and heavily specialised — and why a hand-rolled WGSL approximation lands far from them.

**Diagnosed causes, consolidated across sources:** occupancy loss from VGPR/LDS (tiles, accumulators, double buffers, spills); LDS bank conflicts and LDS-latency dominance; too little parallelism/outstanding-load capacity at small shapes; compiler-emitted redundant or narrow loads (ACO SGPR allocation, aliased views); wave-size mismatch; batch-size-specific shader-variant pathologies; and measurement artifacts (warm caches, warmup-shape mismatch) masquerading as all of the above. No source found attributes such a gap to DRAM, dispatch overhead, or “Vulkan being slow” — and two sources explicitly refute those explanations on measurement.
